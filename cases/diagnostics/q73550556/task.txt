On this shared Ubuntu machine a small container node is modelled on the question "Error no OpenTelemetry when containerd starts. Containerd version 1.6.8": a containerd 1.6.8 logs, at every start, `level=error msg="failed to initialize a tracing processor \"otlp\"" error="no OpenTelemetry endpoint: skip plugin"`. The only answer of the question says that the message is harmless (containerd works and the tracing plugin is optional), and the node here confirms it: nothing is broken. This task is therefore not a repair of a fault: it takes the message as the sign that containerd's tracing is switched off, and asks to switch it on.

The node is a private containerd 1.6.8 with the CRI plugin (CRI socket /run/bench73550556/containerd.sock; its files are below /var/lib/bench73550556 and /run/bench73550556; the machine's own containerd, crictl and runc are not the node's). It runs with containerd's default configuration, /var/lib/bench73550556/etc/containerd.toml (the file containerd reads when it starts), and its log is /run/bench73550556/containerd.log. The commands are /var/lib/bench73550556/bin/nodectl start|stop|restart|status and /var/lib/bench73550556/bin/crictl (crictl for the node's socket), both with sudo. One pod, app-pod, runs on the network of the node; its container app logs one line every half second.

A real OpenTelemetry Collector 0.91.0 (the lab's, /var/lib/bench73550556/otelcol) already runs on this machine with an OTLP receiver over gRPC on 127.0.0.1:4317 and over HTTP on 127.0.0.1:4318, and writes every span it receives, as JSON, to /run/bench73550556/spans.jsonl; its configuration is /var/lib/bench73550556/otelcol/config.yaml, its log is /run/bench73550556/otelcol.log, and it is started and stopped with `sudo /var/lib/bench73550556/bin/otelctl start|stop|restart|status`. Nothing has to be pulled from the internet.

Your task: make containerd 1.6.8 of the node send its traces to this collector, so that the spans of what the node does (the CRI calls: listing pods and images, running a pod, creating and starting a container) arrive in /run/bench73550556/spans.jsonl, without harming the node: the container that runs now must go on running and logging, and new pods must start. The check does not look at the message; it makes random calls of its own after your commands (it lists the pods and the images a random number of times and runs a new pod with a random name and a container) and then looks in the collector's file for the spans of exactly these calls: the right number of ListPodSandbox and ListImages spans, and the RunPodSandbox, CreateContainer and StartContainer spans of its own pod and container, with the pod's and the container's ids. Hiding or removing the message does not help, and spans that were not produced by containerd's own calls are of no use. The solution has to work with the programs of the lab: the lab's files, that is the node's programs (containerd, its shim, runc, ctr), the collector's binary and its configuration, the tools in bin and the definitions of the pod, stay as they are; containerd.toml and the restart of containerd are for you, and the collector must keep running, the one of the lab, without a restart. Use the lab's tools, curl, python3 and the standard shell tools; do not use docker or podman.

The current user has passwordless sudo. Unless stated otherwise, assume container-runtime and system commands need sudo.

Return the commands needed to complete the task.
