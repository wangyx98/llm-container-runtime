On this shared Ubuntu machine a small container node is modelled on the question "Why am I getting 'failed to flush chunk' when sending container logs to syslogs with fluent-bit?": since the host moved from Docker to containerd, Fluent Bit 2.0.3 is meant to send the container logs to syslog, and its own log is full of `[ warn] [engine] failed to flush chunk '...', retry in 9 seconds: task_id=..., input=tail.0 > output=syslog.0`, whatever else is tried. Nothing in the question says why.

The node is a private containerd 1.6.17 with the CRI plugin (CRI socket /run/bench74310243/containerd.sock; its files are below /var/lib/bench74310243 and /run/bench74310243; the machine's own containerd, crictl and runc are not the node's). The commands are /var/lib/bench74310243/bin/nodectl start|stop|restart|status and /var/lib/bench74310243/bin/crictl (crictl for the node's socket), both with sudo. One pod, app-pod, runs on the network of the node; its container app logs one line every half second (`bench74310243 TOKEN N`, N counting up). The log is in the kubelet's layout under /var/lib/bench74310243/var/log: containerd writes the CRI file pods/default_app-pod_uid74310243/app/0.log (`TIMESTAMP stdout F TEXT`), and containers/app-pod_default_app-<container id>.log is the symlink Fluent Bit reads.

Fluent Bit 2.0.3 (the program of the lab, /var/lib/bench74310243/fb/bin/fluent-bit) runs with the configuration of the question, /var/lib/bench74310243/etc/fluent-bit.conf: a tail input over containers/*.log with the stock parser cri (kubernetes filter left out: the lab has no API server), and a syslog output to 127.0.0.1 port 514 over UDP in rfc5424 with the syslog_*_key options of the question. It is started and stopped with `sudo /var/lib/bench74310243/bin/flbctl start|stop|restart|status` (the configuration is read at the start; its log, in debug, is /run/bench74310243/fluent-bit.log, and its monitoring counters are at http://127.0.0.1:20310/api/v1/metrics). The machine's syslog daemon is modelled by the lab's own small syslog daemon, a stand-in for rsyslog and not rsyslog: its configuration is /var/lib/bench74310243/etc/syslogd.conf (one directive per line, `#` starts a comment, `listen udp ADDRESS:PORT` receives syslog datagrams on that address; it is read when the daemon starts), it is started and stopped with `sudo /var/lib/bench74310243/bin/syslogctl start|stop|restart|status`, and it writes every datagram it receives as a JSON line (time, peer, raw text) to /run/bench74310243/syslog.jsonl. Nothing has to be pulled from the internet.

Your task: find out why Fluent Bit fails to flush, and make the container's log lines reach the syslog daemon through Fluent Bit, as valid RFC 5424 messages. The check lets the container go on logging for 18 seconds and judges the lines logged from the fourth second to the second before the end: each of them must have arrived in the daemon's log exactly once and in the order of the CRI file, as an RFC 5424 message (PRI, version 1, TIMESTAMP, HOSTNAME, APP-NAME, PROCID, MSGID, STRUCTURED-DATA, then the text, with or without a BOM) whose text is the line's text and whose TIMESTAMP is the CRI line's own; nothing else may arrive in the daemon's log. In the same time Fluent Bit's log may not show another `failed to flush chunk` or `cannot be retried` for the syslog output, and its monitoring counters must show that its syslog output sent at least as many records as there are judged lines. Lines logged before the fix are not required. The solution has to keep working when Fluent Bit and the daemon are restarted with the tools above; the fix may not be a lower Log_Level, may not hide the warnings, and may not replace Fluent Bit or the daemon by something else (a shipper, a listener or a logger of your own does not count: the lab's files, that is the node's programs, Fluent Bit's binary and the daemon's program, the tools in bin and the definitions of the pod, stay as they are; the two configuration files and the restart of the two programs are for you). Fluent Bit, the daemon and the container must be the lab's processes, running, when the check ends. Use the lab's tools, curl, python3 and the standard shell tools; do not use docker or podman.

The current user has passwordless sudo. Unless stated otherwise, assume container-runtime and system commands need sudo.

Return the commands needed to complete the task.
